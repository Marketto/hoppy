# 0009. iPhone/iPad: SwiftUI shell over the Rust core, unsigned .ipa sideloaded with Sideloadly
Status: Proposed (PoC; becomes Accepted after the CI job `ios` is green and the manual test
plan in `docs/platforms/ios.md` passed on an iPad Air 2022 with iPadOS 18)
Date: 2026-10-15

## Context
The owner wants Hoppy on an iPad (iPad Air 2022, iPadOS 18) at **zero cost**: no Apple
Developer Program membership, so no TestFlight, App Store, Developer ID or paid
entitlements. His Mac (2015, macOS 12) cannot run a current Xcode; builds must come from the
GitHub macOS runner. The free route is a **free Apple ID "personal team"** signature applied
on the user's side by a sideloading tool (Sideloadly, from Windows or macOS), valid 7 days.

What a free personal team cannot have, and what that forces:
- **No multicast entitlement** (`com.apple.developer.networking.multicast` needs Apple's
  approval): the app can neither send nor receive multicast/broadcast UDP, so spec §2's
  discovery does not work as is.
- **No App Groups, iCloud or push**: the Share extension cannot share storage or defaults
  with the app; no remote wake-up.
- **No background listening**: iOS suspends the app shortly after it leaves the screen.
- Sideloaded apps need **Developer Mode** (iOS/iPadOS 16+) and expire after 7 days.

Options for the engine: a 4th implementation of the protocol in Swift (Network.framework), or
the Rust core through a C ABI (the Android Kotlin port was a PoC shortcut, ADR 0002, whose
stated goal is one engine).

## Decision
- **Engine: the Rust core**, never a 4th protocol implementation. `crates/hoppy-ffi`
  (staticlib + rlib) wraps `hoppy-core` behind a hand-written C header
  (`crates/hoppy-ffi/include/hoppy.h`): one opaque node, JSON in, JSON events out through one
  C callback. No UniFFI/cbindgen (not needed for ~10 functions, and the build stays offline-
  friendly). Built for `aarch64-apple-ios`, `aarch64-apple-ios-sim`, `x86_64-apple-ios` into
  `HoppyFFI.xcframework` with a module map (`import HoppyFFI`).
- **Discovery without multicast: the unicast sweep** (spec §2.1), client behaviour in
  `hoppy-core` (`Sweep`), **no wire change**: the node sends unicast `query` to every host of
  its private /24 (at most 256 targets; never public, CGNAT or link-local ranges; never
  cellular, VPN or virtual adapters; the default-route interface when there is one), paced,
  every 10 s, plus to seeds (recently seen peer IPs, IPs typed by the
  user). Spec §2 rule 3 already makes every receiver answer a `query` with a unicast
  `announce` *and* record the sender: so the iPad finds receivers and receivers list the
  iPad. While receiving, the iPad queries its known peers every 5 s (keeps both sides fresh)
  and sends `bye` to them by unicast when it stops.
  - Desktop send-only processes (`hoppyw send`, `hoppy send`/`peers` without `--seed`) bind
    an ephemeral port and never hear the iPad, so they **sweep too** (one /24 at start, then
    every 10 s while the picker is open; `--no-sweep` / `HOPPY_NO_SWEEP=1` turn it off). Android shares one node on 47470 and hears the
    iPad's sweep queries directly; Kotlin and Python are unchanged.
- **UI: SwiftUI** (iOS 15+, iPhone + iPad) in `apps/ios`, project generated by **XcodeGen**
  (`project.yml`, no committed `.pbxproj`). One screen: mascot, "Pronto a ricevere", nearby
  devices, "Invia file" (Files picker, Photos picker), plus "Open in Hoppy" (document types).
  Purikura palette, Night in dark mode, IT + EN, WCAG AA checked by `tools/contrast.py`.
- **Receiving only in the foreground**: the node starts when the scene is active and stops
  (with `bye`) when it goes to the background; a running transfer gets the ~30 s of
  `beginBackgroundTask`. Accept/Decline is an in-app alert, Decline is the cancel/default
  action, no answer in 110 s = declined (invariant 3). Files go to the app's Documents
  (`UIFileSharingEnabled` + `LSSupportsOpeningDocumentsInPlace`: Files → On My iPad → Hoppy),
  with Open (Quick Look), Share and Show in Files.
- **"Condividi → Hoppy": a self-contained Share extension** (`HoppyShare.appex`). It stages
  the shared items into its own temporary folder (APFS clones, nothing in memory: the
  extension limit is ~120 MB), runs its own send-only node on an **ephemeral** UDP port (never
  stealing 47470 from the app), sweeps, and streams the files. No URL-scheme hand-off to the
  app (extensions cannot open URLs without responder-chain hacks). Its defaults are separate
  from the app's, so it keeps its own id and uses the device model ("iPad") as its name.
- **Distribution: `Hoppy-ios-unsigned.ipa`** (`Payload/Hoppy.app` with
  `PlugIns/HoppyShare.appex`), built with `CODE_SIGNING_ALLOWED=NO` by `release.yml` (job
  `ios`, no longer gated by `ENABLE_IOS`) and listed in `SHA256SUMS.txt`. The user installs it
  with Sideloadly and a free Apple ID (which re-signs the app and the extension), trusts the
  Apple ID in Settings and turns on Developer Mode. No entitlements are requested at all.
- **Verification without a Mac**: pure logic is in Rust and tested on Linux (`Sweep`
  targets/schedule, end-to-end sweep discovery, FFI tests through the C entry points);
  `tools/ios_check.py` checks strings, plists, project paths and header usage statically; CI
  job `ios` builds the xcframework, the unsigned device app and runs the Swift FFI smoke
  tests on a simulator, with failures re-emitted as annotations
  (`tools/ci_annotate_xcode.py`).

## Consequences
- One engine on iOS, desktop and (eventually) Android; protocol changes need no Swift work.
- The sweep adds ~250 small unicast datagrams every 10 s while the app (or a desktop picker)
  is open; on a /16 office network only the iPad's own /24 is swept, so peers elsewhere need
  "Aggiungi per indirizzo IP". Receivers that only hear the iPad through its sweep (Android
  in send-only mode) see it with up to ~10 s delay.
- The 7-day signature, Developer Mode and the trust step are user-visible costs, documented
  in the portal, README and release notes. A Developer Program membership later allows
  TestFlight, the multicast entitlement (then the sweep can be turned off on iOS) and an App
  Group for a shared name.
- No background receiving: the sender sees "unreachable" when the iPad's Hoppy is not open;
  documented as a product fact (`docs/platforms/ios.md`).

## Addendum (2026-10-15): v1 identity of the Share extension, updates
- **Identity with protocol v1 (ADR 0010).** The app and the extension each keep a persistent
  device key and trust store in their own `Library/Application Support/Hoppy` (excluded from
  backups) and the device id is bound to that key (new key ⇒ new id, never KeyChanged on
  other devices). Sharing one identity between app and extension was rejected: App Groups are
  not available to free personal teams (Xcode refuses the capability for them; we could not
  confirm that Sideloadly registers groups), `keychain-access-groups` needs the signer's team
  prefix, which an unsigned .ipa cannot know and Sideloadly rebuilds from the provisioning
  profile, and handing the files to the app needs the responder-chain URL hack. So the
  extension is **a separate device**, «<model> (Condividi)», with its own stable id, key and
  trust store: zero cost, nothing to configure, no KeyChanged; it is verified separately and
  shows the 4 emoji with «Coincide» / «Più tardi», like the app (it never receives). With a paid account later, an App Group can merge the two identities.
- **Updates at zero cost.** The portal publishes an AltStore-format source,
  `sidestore.json`, built by `tools/site.py` from the latest release (portal rebuilt by
  `release.yml` after publishing): users of SideStore (free, re-signs on the device every
  week) get automatic renewal and updates. The app checks `releases/latest` at most daily and
  shows «Hoppy X disponibile — aggiorna da SideStore o reinstalla con Sideloadly» with a link
  to the portal; it never downloads (a sideloaded app cannot replace itself).
